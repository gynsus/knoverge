# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone others can work with. Assume the person you are replying to is acting in good faith, and give them the benefit of the doubt you would want.

## What we expect

- Discuss the work, not the person. "This function does not handle an empty list" is a review. "You clearly did not think" is not.
- Accept that people have different experience, languages and time zones. English is the project language, and it is not everyone's first one. Answer what someone meant, not how they spelled it.
- Say what you mean plainly, and say when you are unsure.
- When you disagree, make the case. When a decision is made, let it be made.
- Respect a "no" the first time, whether it is about a feature, a review or a conversation.

## What we will not accept

- Harassment, in the repository or anywhere else it follows a participant here.
- Insults, demeaning comments, and attacks on someone's identity or background.
- Sexual attention or imagery.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or repeatedly ignoring a maintainer's direction.

This applies to issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments and any other project space, and to private contact that arises from participating here.

## If something goes wrong

Report it privately to a maintainer through GitHub. Say what happened and where, and include links if you have them. A report will be read by as few people as possible, and we will not share your name with the person reported without asking you first.

Maintainers will respond. Depending on what happened, that may be a private word, a request to edit or withdraw a comment, a temporary block, or a permanent ban. We will say what we decided to the person who reported it.

Maintainers are held to this document like everyone else. A maintainer who breaks it should be reported to another maintainer.

## Scope

This applies to everyone taking part: contributors, reviewers, maintainers and people opening their first issue.
